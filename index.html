Disability and Wellness Research Collaborative

Health, wellbeing, and disability justice

We study the social drivers of health (e.g., housing, social inclusion, discrimination, and access to care) and the systems that create and sustain health disparities for disabled and neurodivergent people. We use that evidence to push for change in policies, services, and communities. Research about disabled people must be conducted with disabled people. As such, community members and organizations are our partners at every stage of the work.

Our guiding principle: “Nothing about us without us.”

Research

Our current work centers on social inclusion, access to care, and the social drivers of health for disabled, neurodivergent, and gender diverse people. We pair national survey and hospital discharge data with community-based and qualitative studies, and we co-create our studies with the people they are about.

Autism, inclusion, and mental wellbeing

Social inclusion is the foundation of lifelong wellbeing.

  • Meaningful Social Inclusion study

    A community-based, mixed methods study of social inclusion and mental wellbeing among autistic adolescents and young adults. Autistic community members helped design it.

    Example publicationMcMaughan et al. (2024). Meaningful social inclusion and mental well-being among autistic adolescents and emerging adults: Protocol for a community-based mixed methods study. JMIR Research Protocols. Read McMaughan et al. (2024) in JMIR Research Protocols

  • Mental health hospitalizations

    Using national hospital discharge data, we examine how often autistic youth and young adults are hospitalized for mental health reasons and what those stays cost. High rates are a sign that community-based mental health care is missing.

    Example publicationMcMaughan et al. (2023). Mental health–related hospitalizations among adolescents and emerging adults with autism in the United States. Autism. Read McMaughan et al. (2023) in Autism

    Finding: Autistic young people were almost 11 times more likely to be hospitalized for mental health reasons than young people with other complex and chronic conditions, and twice as likely as their peers without those conditions. These stays cost an estimated $106 million in 2016.

  • Immigrant families and autism services

    Interviews with immigrant fathers of autistic children about what helped and what got in the way when they looked for services.

    Example publicationImanpour, McGehee, & McMaughan (2025). “As immigrants we are all lost in our autism journey.” Research in Autism. Read Imanpour, McGehee, & McMaughan (2025) in Research in Autism

    Finding: In interviews with 25 Afghan and Iranian immigrant fathers of autistic children, fathers described stress related to parenting, bonding with their children, and cultural stigma. They faced barriers to autism services because of limited social support and the complexity of the U.S. health care system.

Disability, gender, and sexual diversity

Disabled LGBTQ+ people face compounding barriers in health care. We document where the system falls short and what needs to change.

  • National Survey on Health and Disability

    Discrimination and unmet health care needs among disabled adults, including transgender and gender diverse people.

    Example publicationMulcahy et al. (2022). Gender identity, disability, and unmet healthcare needs among disabled people living in the community in the United States. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. Read Mulcahy et al. (2022) in International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health

    Finding: Transgender people with disabilities are more likely to have unmet health care needs than cisgender people with disabilities.

  • Experiences in health care

    Disabled sexual and gender minority patients, including autistic LGBTQ+ people, describe their interactions with health care providers.

    Example publicationMulcahy et al. (2025). Experiences of patients with disabilities and sexual or gender minority status during healthcare interactions. Journal of Public Health. Read Mulcahy et al. (2025) in Journal of Public Health

    Finding: Almost three in four (72.2%) LGBTQ+ people with disabilities avoided discussing their gender or sexual identity with their health care providers. Many described fear, distrust, and avoidance of care (40.1%) or having treatment dismissed or denied (30.5%).

  • COVID-19 and delayed care

    Delayed and skipped care during the COVID-19 pandemic, and what it meant for the wellbeing of disabled people of different gender identities.

    Example publicationMcMaughan et al. (2024). Gender diversity, disability, and well-being: Impact of delayed and foregone care because of COVID-19. LGBT Health. Read McMaughan et al. (2024) in LGBT Health

    Finding: During COVID-19, gender diverse disabled people were over four times more likely to delay care and three times more likely to go without care than cisgender disabled people.

Structural barriers to care

Policy, cost, and where people live can keep them from the care they need. These barriers are systemic, and they are modifiable.

  • Rural community resilience

    Urban and rural differences in COVID-19 and mental health hospitalizations in Oklahoma.

    Example publicationKnutson, Jensen, Parker, & McMaughan (2025). A case for rural community resilience. Journal of Rural Mental Health. Read Knutson, Jensen, Parker, & McMaughan (2025) in Journal of Rural Mental Health

    Finding: Compared with urban residents, rural Oklahomans had 7% higher odds of hospitalization for COVID-19 but 21% lower odds of hospitalization for mental health concerns.

  • Medicaid policy and public health

    The public health effects of Medicaid cuts in Oklahoma, including lost access to care.

    Example presentationParker et al. (2025). Policy vs. patients: Unpacking the public health impact of Medicaid cuts in Oklahoma. APHA Annual Meeting and Expo.

  • Gender-affirming care and policy

    State policy, cost, safety, and discrimination as barriers to gender-affirming care, including hormone therapy.

    Example presentationMcGehee et al. (2023). Access to gender affirming care and preemptive policy: A family impact policy analysis and state case study. APHA Annual Meeting and Expo.

  • Unmet mental health care needs

    Mental health disparities and unmet mental health care needs among disabled adults with ADHD.

    Example presentationMcMaughan et al. (2024). The adults are not OK!: The mental health disparities and unmet mental health care needs of disabled adults with ADHD. APHA Annual Meeting and Expo.

Social drivers of health

Housing, food security, and everyday discrimination are health issues. They affect wellbeing long before anyone sees a provider.

Housing instability

  • Housing instability and wellbeing Current

    Housing instability and unequal housing among disabled people, especially those with marginalized sexual and gender identities, using National Survey on Health and Disability data.

    Example presentationMcMaughan et al. (2025). Navigating instability: Housing instability, well-being, and intersectionality among disabled people. APHA Annual Meeting and Expo. Journal articles in progress.

  • Residential care and safety

    Victimization of disabled and older adults in unethical, unlicensed small residential care homes, and the case for whole-system disruption.

    Example publicationMcMaughan et al. (2024). Victimization in unethical unlicensed small residential care homes in the United States: The case for whole system disruption. Journal of Aging & Social Policy. Read McMaughan et al. (2024) in Journal of Aging & Social Policy

    Recommendation: Ending victimization in unlicensed care homes requires change at the federal, state, and local levels, including a federal definition of small residential care homes, state registration, stronger oversight, and clear ways to report abuse.

  • Supportive housing for older disabled adults

    Mapping research on board and care homes in the United States through an age-friendly communities lens.

    Example publicationMcMaughan et al. (2021). Supportive housing for older adults with disabilities: Mapping the literature on board and care homes. Journal of Aging and Environment. Read McMaughan et al. (2021) in Journal of Aging and Environment

    Finding: Board and care homes are often affordable, but the research shows they can be unsafe, vary in the services they provide, are mostly unregulated, and may offer few chances to socialize.

Food security, stress, and substance use

  • Minority stress and tobacco use

    Neurodivergence, minority stress, and tobacco use among LGBTQ+ young adults.

    Example publicationVogel et al. (2025). Neurodivergence, minority stress, and tobacco use in a sample of US sexual and gender minoritized young adults. Substance Use & Addiction Journal. Read Vogel et al. (2025) in Substance Use & Addiction Journal

    Finding: Over a third (36.1%) of LGBTQ+ young adults in the study were neurodivergent. Neurodivergent young adults had higher odds of mental health symptoms, cigarette use, and e-cigarette use.

  • Food insecurity and substance use

    The prevalence of food insecurity and substance use among transgender and gender diverse college students.

    Example publicationKane et al. (2026). Prevalence of substance use and food insecurity among transgender and gender diverse college students. Journal of American College Health. Read Kane et al. (2026) in Journal of American College Health

    Finding: Transgender and gender diverse students had higher odds of using hallucinogens, cannabis, prescription stimulants, and cocaine than their cisgender peers, and were more likely to report problems caused by their substance use.

Our team

Current members of the Collaborative.

Directors

DJ McMaughan, PhD

they/them

Director · Professor of Public Health

Dr. DJ McMaughan is a Professor of Public Health and the Director of the Disability and Wellness Research Collaborative. Their research starts from lived experience and community priorities and focuses on the structural inequities that keep disabled and neurodivergent people from the health and social care they need. Their interests include (1) participatory and statistical methods for justice-oriented public health; (2) social inclusion and relationships as drivers of health; (3) autism, neurodivergence, and intersecting identities; and (4) housing, employment, and other social drivers of health. DJ earned a PhD in Health Services Research from Texas A&M University and mentors students to treat lived experience as central to rigorous research.

Elliot Parker, MPH

he/him

Co-Director

Elliot Parker is Co-Director of the Disability and Wellness Research Collaborative and a PhD student in Community Health Sciences. The thread throughout his work is applying justice-oriented, community-engaged research methodology to investigate systemic barriers to health and wellbeing stemming from stigma and marginalization. His current research projects focus on social inclusion and housing instability among queer and neurodivergent populations.

Research affiliates

Emily Abel, PhD

Research Affiliate · Assistant Professor in Public Health

Dr. Emily Abel earned her PhD in Human Development and Family Science from Purdue University in 2022 and has extensive training in pediatric sleep health, developmental assessment, and developmental disabilities. Her research focuses on measuring, understanding, and promoting sleep health for autistic children and their families. Her current work uses telehealth approaches (non-invasive, remote monitoring) to make sleep research more accessible, so that children and caregivers can decide what better sleep and quality of life look like for them.

Dr. Sara Imanpour is an Assistant Professor of Health Administration at Penn State Harrisburg.
